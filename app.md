## Description (as published)

Halatto is an iOS app for a vintage photo filter. It makes a photo you already have look like it was shot on film or on an old camera: a disposable, an instant camera, a plastic toy camera, a 90s point-and-shoot, or a digicam. Use this app when a phone picture is too clean and sharp for the post you want to share.

Phone photos are sharp and clean. Film is not. Halatto puts film color, grain, and the softness of an old lens on the picture, so it looks like a print from a roll of film.

FILM AND OLD-CAMERA LOOKS
Each look recreates a kind of camera, not only a color grade: its film or sensor (color, grain, digital noise) and its lens (softness, vignette, flare, light leaks).
- Apply a 35mm or night-cinema look, with a glow around lights
- Give a photo the warm, grainy look of a disposable camera
- Soften color into an instant-photo look
- Add the dark corners and light leaks of a toy camera
- Match a 90s point-and-shoot, in color or black and white
- Recreate a digicam shot, with flash, noise, and cool whites
- Set the strength, grain, and film age after you pick a look

MAKE A PHOTO LOOK OLD
- Add film grain, dust, fade, and film age
- Mark the print with stains and creases
- Lay in light leaks and a vignette
- Add a camera-style flash
- Stamp the orange date from the day the photo was taken
- Crop the photo and adjust color and light

ONE LOOK FOR A SET OF PHOTOS
Save an edit as your own recipe and use it on other photos. Develop the Roll puts that look on many photos at once, so a trip, a party, or a feed looks like one roll of film.

MORE
- Shoot a new photo in the built-in camera with the look already on
- Make a short before-and-after video of the edit to share
- Reopen an edit later and change the look
- Open a photo from the Photos app with the share sheet
- Edit on your iPhone. No account, and nothing is uploaded.

WHO IT'S FOR
People who want film, disposable, and digicam photos for portraits, friends, travel, night shots, and social posts, without buying film or an old camera.

Every look, filter, and effect is available without a subscription, up to a daily limit. A subscription removes the limit.

Open a photo, pick a vintage photo filter, and share it.

## Editor tools seen in the screenshots (use these exact labels in guides)

- **Looks**: horizontal strip of cameras (Mju 35, Snap 400, Snap Gold, Tunnel 35, Toy 120, Dream 120 …); a Before/After divider on the photo.
- **Aging**: presets Original, Shoebox, Faded print, 70s Mag…; sliders Fade, Sunlight, Yellowing, Unevenness. Plus dust, stains, creases.
- **Light Leak**: presets Original, Soft red, Orange, Golden …; type Edge, Window, Burn; sliders Color, Amount, Position.
- **Flash**: None, Retro, Digital, Tanning; Intensity slider.
- **Date stamp**: off or one of four corners; Direction (horizontal / vertical up / vertical down); Color slider; Size slider. The date comes from the day the photo was taken (e.g. '26 11 19).
- **Develop the Roll**: pick a camera, then "Develop all (N)" applies it to every selected photo.
- **Save as Recipe**: names the current edit ("Recipe 1") so it can be applied to other photos in one tap.
- **Export (before & after video)**: aspect ratio Original, 9:16, 4:5, 1:1; "Share video" button.
- **Camera**: built-in camera with a live look (Mini Instant, Silver IX, Cyber Blue, Digi 2MP …).
- Undo / redo in every tool, crop, color and light adjustments, open from Photos via the share sheet, reopen an edit later.

## Competitive landscape (Nov 2026)

Camera-first apps (shoot with a filter): Huji Cam, Dazz Cam, NOMO CAM, 1998 Cam, Dispo, KAPI, CoreCam, Retrica, a long tail of new "Y2K digicam" and "disposable camera" apps. Editor-first apps: VSCO, RNI Films, Dehancer, Lightroom presets, single-purpose grain and date-stamp apps (Grain+, GrainLab, DateStamper, Timestamper).

Halatto's angle: an **editor for photos you already have** (most competitors are cameras), whole-camera simulation rather than a color LUT, aging + light leaks + date stamp + flash in one place, batch "Develop the Roll", recipes, before/after video, on-device with no account. Use that angle in every page.
