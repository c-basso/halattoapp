How to make your iPhone photos look like film

By Vladimir Ivakhnenko · Updated November 26, 2026, 5 min read

Quick answer

A photo reads as film when four things change together: film color (softer contrast, warm highlights, tinted shadows), grain, an old lens (softness, vignette, flare) and a little fade. The Photos app can only get you part of the way with Warmth, Contrast and Vignette. In Halatto, open the photo, pick a film look such as Titan 38 or Tungsten 800, raise grain and film age a little, and save. It works on photos you already took and edits on your iPhone.

iPhone photos are sharp, clean and evenly lit. That is exactly what film is not. Film has a color palette of its own, visible grain, highlights that roll off softly and a lens that is never perfectly sharp at the edges.

This guide explains what to change and how far to push it, first with the free tools in Photos, then with Halatto, which simulates the whole camera instead of only the color.

What makes a photo look like film

A film look is not one setting. These are the signs people recognize, in the order they matter:

  1. Color: lifted blacks, softer contrast, warm or green-tinted shadows depending on the film stock.
  2. Grain: an even, slightly irregular texture, stronger in shadows and on fast film.
  3. Lens: softness toward the edges, a vignette, flare and the occasional light leak.
  4. Age: fade, a warm shift and a little dust if the print has been in a box.

If you only change color, the photo looks filtered, not shot on film. Grain and lens softness do most of the work.

The free way: edit in the Photos app

Open the photo, tap Edit and use Adjust. Lower Contrast and Brilliance slightly, raise Warmth, drop Highlights, nudge Black Point down, and add a small Vignette. Save.

You will get a warmer, softer picture, but Photos has no grain, no light leaks and no lens softness, so the result usually looks like a filter rather than a print.

Tips for a convincing film look

  • Use the same look on a whole set; a consistent film is what makes a feed look shot on one roll.
  • Slightly underexposed photos take film color better than bright, HDR-heavy ones.
  • Less is more with grain on faces; push it on streets and night shots.
  • Night photos with lights in the frame are perfect for Tungsten 800's halation glow.

Questions people also ask

Can the iPhone camera shoot film photos directly?

Not real film, but the built-in camera in Halatto shows the look live, so the photo comes out already looking like film.

Is there a free film filter on iPhone?

Photos has basic filters and Adjust sliders, which give a warm, faded look but no grain or lens effects. Halatto is free to try with a daily limit.

Do film looks work on old photos and screenshots?

Yes, any photo in your library works. Low-resolution photos often look even more natural with a disposable or digicam look.

Are my photos uploaded?

No. Halatto edits on the iPhone with no account.
