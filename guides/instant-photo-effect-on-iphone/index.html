Polaroid-style filter for iPhone: the instant photo look

Updated November 21, 2026, 5 min read

Quick answer

Instant film has soft contrast, glassy or tinted shadows and pastel highlights. In Halatto pick SX Fold (folding instant: deep blue shadows, lilac highlights), Square 600 (boxy instant: green shadows, pink highlights) or Mini Instant (credit-card prints: clear, bright, a little cool), then crop to a square or portrait and add light aging. Halatto is not affiliated with Polaroid or Fujifilm.

"Polaroid filter" is how most people search for the instant-film look, whichever brand they mean. The look has little to do with the white frame and a lot to do with the color of the chemistry.

Here are the three instant styles, what each looks like, and how to apply them to an iPhone photo.

The three instant looks

LookInspired byCharacter
SX Fold1970s folding instant camerasGlassy, deep blue shadows, lilac highlights.
Square 6001980s–90s boxy instant camerasSoft, green shadows, pink highlights.
Mini InstantModern credit-card size instant filmClear, bright, a little cool.

Why instant photos look the way they do

Instant film develops in the print itself. The chemistry gives low contrast, colors that drift toward cyan, green or magenta, and soft, slightly glowing highlights. Older prints fade further and shift warm.

That is why a plain white border plus a normal filter rarely convinces: the color is what people recognize.

Tips

  • Instant film loves soft window light and overcast days.
  • Lower the look strength slightly on skin for a gentler pastel.
  • Keep the date stamp off; most instant cameras never printed one.

Questions people also ask

Is there an official Polaroid filter?

Polaroid's own app is for its cameras and printers. Halatto offers instant-film styles and is not affiliated with Polaroid.

Can I shoot with the instant look live?

Yes. Open the built-in camera in Halatto and pick Mini Instant or another instant look before you shoot.

Does Halatto add a white instant frame?

Halatto focuses on the film color, grain and aging of the print itself.
