90s photo filter: the point-and-shoot film look on iPhone

Updated November 21, 2026, 5 min read

Quick answer

A 90s photo was usually shot on a 35mm compact or a disposable: crisp but slightly soft color, visible grain, a direct flash indoors and an orange date in the corner. In Halatto, choose Titan 38 (premium 90s compact), Mju 35 (pocket point-and-shoot in black and white) or Snap 400 (disposable), add the Retro flash for indoor shots and the date stamp, then save.

The 90s look is film, not digital: no sensor noise, no crunchy JPEG, but grain, warm skin and a flash that makes everyone glow.

Here is how to pick the right camera for the photo and finish it the way a lab print looked.

Which 90s camera for which photo

  • Titan 38: city trips, portraits, golden hour. Crisp, cool shadows, warm highlights.
  • Mju 35: black and white street and friends. Hard contrast, big grain.
  • Snap 400 and Snap Gold: parties, holidays, the camera that went around the table.

Tips

  • Crop to 3:2, the shape of a 35mm frame.
  • A slight Aging fade makes it look like a print from an album, not a fresh scan.
  • Use one camera for the whole set; 90s albums were one or two rolls.

Questions people also ask

What app gives the 90s filter on TikTok and Instagram?

Many apps do. Halatto gives the 90s point-and-shoot and disposable looks on photos you already took, with flash and the orange date.

Can I make a 90s look in black and white?

Yes. Mju 35 is a black and white pocket point-and-shoot with hard contrast and big grain.

Is the 90s look the same as Y2K?

No. 90s is film; Y2K usually means early digital cameras. See the digicam filter guide for that look.
